# Interview guide — Manila Lepto Prophylaxis Access

**Segment:** Informal-sector workers/vendors in flood-prone Manila barangays (Tondo, Baseco/Port
Area, Sta. Ana, Pandacan) exposed to floodwater during the current epidemic; plus a secondary set of
questions for barangay health-center staff distributing doxycycline. **Target:** 8–12 conversations
(6–8 residents, 2–4 health-center staff). **Channel:** in-person at barangay health centers / relief
distribution points, or via barangay health worker introductions — not yet run this session.

## Opening (1 min)
"I'm trying to understand how people handle getting sick or staying safe after wading through
floodwater during this outbreak. I don't have anything to show you — just want to understand what
actually happened last time."

## Questions — residents
1. Tell me about the last time you were in floodwater during this outbreak. What did you do right after? *(anchors on a real event)*
2. Did you know the doxycycline was free before I mentioned it — and if so, how did you find out? *(→ did/said, channel discovery)*
3. Did you go get it? Where, and how long did it take from exposure to getting there? *(→ did)*
4. If you didn't go — what stopped you? *(→ workaround/failure mode)*
5. Have you ever gone to a health center for this and found it closed or out of medicine? What did you do next? *(→ did, wasted-trip cost)*
6. What do you pay, if anything, out of pocket when you or your family get sick after a flood? *(→ paid)*
7. If your phone got a message saying the nearest place with the medicine was open right now, would you go — and what would stop you? *(anchor on the actual next flood, not a hypothetical want)*
8. Do you have a phone that can receive SMS even without mobile data loaded? *(→ channel feasibility)*

## Questions — barangay health-center / hospital staff
1. Walk me through what happens at your center when doxycycline supply runs low during a surge like this one. *(→ did)*
2. How do you currently let anyone outside your center know your stock or open/closed status? *(→ did, current channel)*
3. If there were a one-tap way to mark "in stock / low / out," how often would you realistically update it during a shift? *(anchor on effort tolerance, not hypothetical enthusiasm)*

## Do not ask
- "Would you use an app that tells you where to get the medicine?" — a `said` at best.
- "How much would you pay for a service like this?" — the medicine and the information are free; ask what they pay today for the *consequences* of not having it (item 6) instead.
