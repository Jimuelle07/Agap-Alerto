# Research brief — Manila Lepto Prophylaxis Access

## 0. Header
- Research date: 2026-09-13
- Input restated: On 2026-09-07 Manila's OIC-City Health Officer reported an "epidemic condition" to
  Mayor Francisco "Isko" Moreno Domagoso after the leptospirosis attack rate jumped from 1.27% to
  3.48% in a week, filling Ospital ng Maynila and Sta. Ana Hospital. In response to flood-driven

## 1. The problem as the people who have it describe it
Officials describe a jurisdiction overwhelmed faster than its own reporting cycle: Manila's health
officer told the mayor the city had crossed into "epidemic condition" the same week the attack rate
nearly tripled, and by the next day hospitals were turning away or straining to admit more patients
(Ospital ng Maynila, Sta. Ana Hospital, Justice Jose Abad Santos General Hospital all near or at
capacity) — source: manilatimes.net, 2026-09-07, via search synthesis; mb.com.ph, 2026-09-08, via
search synthesis. Nationally, DOH's own epidemiologist framed the risk as a lagging one: "infections
may surge as another wave of cases could be experienced in two weeks due to the disease's incubation
period" — DOH Undersecretary Gloria Balboa, quoted in philstar.com, 2026-09-04 (opened directly).
The people actually exposed are described structurally, not individually, in the literature: outbreaks
"disproportionately affect impoverished communities in informal, flood-prone settlements where poor
sanitation, unsafe housing, and limited healthcare access compound vulnerability" (CDC Emerging
Infectious Diseases, "Rethinking Leptospirosis Prevention, the Philippines," Vol. 32 No. 3, March
2026, via search synthesis — direct fetch blocked by bot-check).

| Adjacent problem | How it differs | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dengue / flu misdiagnosis during flood season | Same febrile presentation delays lepto-specific care, but the fix is diagnostic triage, not access to prophylaxis | search synthesis of PMC clinical-delay literature, 2026 |

| General flood relief logistics (food, evacuation) | Distinct workflow and city offices; only overlaps at the same barangay evacuation centers | [assumption] — not directly sourced this session, inferred from city response structure |

**Recommended segment: informal workers and market vendors in Manila's flood-exposed, low-income
barangays (e.g., Tondo, Baseco/Port Area) who were in floodwater during the current epidemic window**
— because this is the one group every sourced figure converges on: 72% of Metro Manila cases are
flood-exposure-linked and 55% of infections are among the unemployed/informal-worker population
(philstar.com, 2026-09-04, opened directly), and occupational literature separately names market
vendors and casual/manual laborers as the highest-exposure occupations (search synthesis, CDC EID /
MDPI structural-roots papers, 2026).

## 5. Root cause candidates
1. **Reporting flows the wrong direction.** Manila (like the rest of the country) has SMS/barangay
   rapid-reporting infrastructure, but it is built to push case counts *up* to health workers and
   DOH, not to push "go here, now, it's stocked" guidance *down* to an exposed resident within the
   68 clinically relevant hours. Five-whys: cases surge → hospitals fill → because prophylaxis
   wasn't taken in time → because residents didn't know where/when to get it → because the existing
   alert infrastructure is a surveillance tool, not a resident-facing access tool → because no one
   owns the "last mile" of a distributed government benefit under time pressure. Evidence favors
   this: R-008 explicitly describes the existing channel's direction. **Ranked #1.**
2. **Trust friction the city itself created.** DOH's public warning against self-medicating with
   doxycycline (R-010) is medically correct but, without an equally loud "here is how to get it FOR
   FREE, SUPERVISED, TODAY" counter-message reaching the same people, may suppress uptake among
   exactly the population that most needs it. **Ranked #2** — plausible and specific, but this
   session found no source directly measuring suppressed uptake; it is inferred, not observed.
3. **Structural exposure (housing, drainage, occupation).** The deepest root cause, per the academic
   literature (R-009), is that this segment lives and works in the flood exposure itself. **Ranked
   #3 for THIS intervention** — real, but not addressable by a software/access tool on the epidemic's
   timescale; it belongs in the parking lot as the thing a screen cannot fix.

### 6.4 Positioning evidence
- "unlike the general info campaign" → the campaign broadcasts awareness city-wide without telling
  an individual which of the 44+7 sites is open and stocked *for them, today* — R-001.

  network already flows information about cases; the same rails can carry stock/hours guidance
  outward instead of only case counts inward — R-008.

- Other systems will need to call into it: yes, plausibly — city health-center reporting systems; [assumption].

### 11.2 `[gap: interview]` — questions only a conversation can answer (feeds phase-2 guide)
1. "Last time it flooded near you, what did you actually do in the first 24 hours?"
2. "Did you know the doxycycline was free before I asked you, or did you find out some other way — and how?"
3. "Have you gone to a health center for something during a flood and found it closed or out of the medicine? What happened next?"
4. "What do you pay, if anything, out of pocket right now when you or your family get sick after wading through floodwater?"
5. "If your phone buzzed with a message telling you the nearest place with the medicine was open right now, would you go — and what would stop you?"
6. "Who in your barangay do you actually ask first when something like this happens — a neighbor, the barangay health worker, or someone else?"
7. "Do you have a phone that can receive SMS even when you have no mobile data loaded?"
8. "As a barangay health worker: how would you actually update whether your center still has stock — what's the least effort you'd tolerate doing that every day?"

### 11.3 Where the evidence contradicts the team's input
- The user's framing centers the mayor's "intensified public information campaign" as the response
  already underway. The evidence in R-008 and root cause #1 suggests this general-broadcast approach
  is structurally different from — and does not replace — a personal, exposure-and-location-specific
  channel; the two are complementary, not duplicative, but this brief is adversarial to treating the
  existing campaign as sufficient.
